# Rustorrent 0.2 Release Candidate 6

The sixth release candidate for 0.2 makes seeding behave like qBittorrent's engine, libtorrent: no time spent on peers that already have everything, and better information passed to the rest of the swarm.

The app is ad-hoc signed for integrity. It is **not Developer ID signed or Apple notarized**, so on first launch macOS may ask you to allow it: right-click the app, choose **Open**, or allow it in **System Settings › Privacy & Security**.

## Seeding

A seed can only upload to peers that are still downloading. On a popular torrent most peers are seeds too, and rc.5 spent its effort on them: it kept seed-to-seed connections open for up to two minutes and dialled the same seeds again every few seconds. In three hours on the Ubuntu 26.04.1 torrent it opened 34,000 connections, nearly all to seeds.

- **Seed-to-seed connections close at once.** When both sides have every piece, there is nothing to exchange.
- **Known seeds are not dialled while seeding.** Rustorrent remembers seeds from their pieces, from BEP 21 `upload_only`, and from the seed flag in peer exchange. If you select more files and download again, seeds are used again.
- **Rustorrent says when it only uploads.** The extension handshake carries BEP 21 `upload_only`, so other seeds can close the connection from their side too. A transfer that finishes sends it then.

In a 10-minute run next to rc.5, seeding the same file on the same network, this release opened 441 connections where rc.5 opened 1,591, held no seed connections where rc.5 held up to 58, and uploaded 16.8 MB where rc.5 uploaded nothing.

How much a seed uploads still depends on the swarm. The Ubuntu torrent has about 1,260 seeds for 60 downloaders, so every seed gets only a small share; a torrent with more downloaders than seeds uploads far more.

## Peer exchange

Peer exchange (BEP 11) now works the way libtorrent's does:

- **Only connected peers are listed,** each at the address that accepts connections: the address Rustorrent dialled, or for a peer that connected in, its IP with the port from its handshake. rc.5 sent 50 addresses picked from every peer it had ever heard of.
- **Each peer carries its flags:** seed, uTP, encryption, and whether it accepts connections. Other clients use them to skip seeds and to choose uTP.
- **Messages carry changes,** the peers added and dropped since the last message to that peer.
- **Flags from other peers are read.** Peers they mark as seeds are remembered as seeds.

## Validation

- **Rust:** 545 unit tests, 9 process tests and 21 transport tests, on Rust 1.99 and with a compile check on Rust 1.89.
- **Real processes:** 11 transfer, crash-recovery and hole-punching scenarios, including a new one: a seed advertises `upload_only` and closes its connection to another seed.
- **libtorrent interoperability:** downloads and uploads over uTP.
- **Transmission interoperability:** seeding on the local network, uploading while downloading, and encrypted transfers.
- **Real swarm:** the Ubuntu 26.04.1 torrent, seeded side by side with rc.5.
- **Browser flows** with WCAG A/AA accessibility checks.
- **CI** covers Linux, macOS and Windows, Rust 1.89, dependency advisories and the license notice.
