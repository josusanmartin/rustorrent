# Rustorrent 0.2 Release Candidate 5

The fifth release candidate for 0.2 fixes three problems that showed up next to qBittorrent on the same network: transfers that found almost no peers, magnet links that never finished loading, and no uploading at all.

- **Linux x86_64:** `rustorrent-0.2.0-rc.5-linux-x86_64.tar.gz` (needs OpenSSL 3)
- ZIP archives of the app and `SHA256SUMS.txt` are attached below. The app requires macOS 11 or newer.

The app is ad-hoc signed for integrity. It is **not Developer ID signed or Apple notarized**, so on first launch macOS may ask you to allow it: right-click the app, choose **Open**, or allow it in **System Settings › Privacy & Security**.

## Finding peers

Many trackers hand out only a few peers. Ubuntu's tracker currently gives every client one, so most peers come from the DHT. Rustorrent's DHT rarely got started:

- **Replies are no longer discarded.** Answers from nodes whose ID does not match their address (BEP 42) were thrown away. That included the one bootstrap router that answers on many networks, and about a third of all nodes. BEP 42 now only decides which nodes Rustorrent keeps in its own table, as in libtorrent.
- **Searches start right away.** Bootstrap routers reply with a single node. Searches now start from those nodes instead of waiting until they are verified, and `dht.libtorrent.org` was added as a router.
- **Silent nodes no longer stall searches.** About a quarter of nodes never answer. A search no longer waits 30 seconds for each one, and retries every few seconds until the DHT has enough nodes.

On the Ubuntu 26.04.1 torrent, Rustorrent went from 1 peer to more than 500 within 15 seconds. It now reaches 10 peers in 3 to 12 seconds from a fresh start.

## Magnet links

Metadata was requested from one peer at a time, from the trackers first and then the DHT, and the tracker step often used up the whole time. Peers from the link, its trackers and the DHT are now gathered together and asked 12 at a time. A magnet that took 70 seconds, or never finished, now loads in about 15.

## Uploading

Rustorrent uploaded nothing on networks where qBittorrent uploaded a little. Three causes, all fixed:

- **uTP comes first.** Like libtorrent, Rustorrent now tries uTP before TCP. Many home routers let peers reply over uTP but block incoming TCP, and other clients only connect back over uTP to peers they know support it.
- **Peers learn where to connect.** The extension handshake now includes the listening port, so other clients can pass Rustorrent's address on to the swarm. It also includes the client name, so peers show **Rustorrent 0.2.0-rc.5** instead of "Retriever".
- **Incoming peers get their own slots.** They were turned away whenever outgoing connections, mostly to seeds, had filled every slot.

In a side-by-side run on the same swarm, Rustorrent uploaded 162 MB in 11 minutes and libtorrent 17 MB.

## Connections per transfer

The Balanced profile now allows 100 connections per transfer and 500 in total, the same as qBittorrent. It previously allowed 30 and 200. Change the per-transfer limit in **Settings › Connections › Connections per transfer**. It applies at once and is remembered after a restart. Choosing a peer profile goes back to that profile's value. The `--max-peers-torrent` flag and config files still take precedence.

Conservative now allows 30 per transfer and 120 in total, and Aggressive 200 and 1,000. The extra outgoing connections opened while nobody could connect in are gone, since the new defaults already exceed them.

## Validation

- **Rust:** 543 unit tests, 9 process tests and 21 transport tests.
- **Real processes:** 10 transfer, crash-recovery and hole-punching scenarios.
- **libtorrent interoperability:** downloads and uploads over uTP.
- **Transmission interoperability:** seeding on the local network, uploading while downloading, and encrypted transfers. The encrypted upload test fails intermittently on macOS, as it did before this release; a fix is being tracked separately.
- **Real swarm:** the Ubuntu 26.04.1 torrent, run side by side with libtorrent 2.1.
- **Browser flows** with WCAG A/AA accessibility checks.
- **CI** covers Linux, macOS and Windows, Rust 1.89, dependency advisories and the license notice.
